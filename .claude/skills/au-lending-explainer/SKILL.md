# Australian Lending Explainer

Turn Australian home lending topics into clear, accurate, compliant explainers. The user is a home lending specialist who creates education content as @FinancewithFrancis. Write for his audience, not for him: he knows the fundamentals.

## Before writing

1. Read `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` if it exists, especially Brand Voice and Compliance Guardrails.
2. Read `references/figures.md`. Every figure there carries a "last checked" date.
3. If any figure you plan to use was last checked more than 90 days ago, or the topic is policy that changes often (scheme caps, grants, stamp duty, rates), search the web for the official source and confirm before using it. Update `references/figures.md` with the new figure, source and date.
4. Never state a figure you cannot source. Say "check the current figure at [official source]" instead.

## Compliance rules (non-negotiable)

- General information only. Never tell a specific person what to do with their situation, never say they will or won't be approved, never estimate a specific person's borrowing power as if it were an assessment.
- Worked examples use clearly hypothetical people and round numbers, and say so.
- Never recommend a specific lender, product or rate. Never compare the user's employer favourably or unfavourably against competitors.
- Never use real customer stories, even anonymised.
- End public content with a short general-information note, for example: "General information only, not financial or credit advice. Consider your own situation and speak to a licensed professional."
- Flag anything that depends on state (stamp duty, grants) and default to Victoria unless told otherwise.

## Topics and how to explain them

**Borrowing power and serviceability.** Lenders assess repayments at the loan rate plus the APRA serviceability buffer, not at the actual rate. Explain the levers: income (and how lenders shade bonus, overtime, rental and self-employed income), living expenses (declared vs benchmark, lenders use the higher), existing debts, credit card limits (assessed on the limit, not the balance), HELP debt, dependants, and loan term. Show the effect of each lever directionally rather than claiming exact lender numbers, since every lender's calculator differs.

**Deposits and LMI.** Explain LVR, why 80% LVR is the usual LMI threshold, that LMI protects the lender not the borrower, and how it can be capitalised. Mention professions that some lenders waive LMI for only in general terms.

**5% Deposit Scheme (Home Guarantee Scheme).** Government guarantee lets eligible first home buyers buy with a 5% deposit without LMI. Cover eligibility at a high level, the price caps by area, and that it goes through participating lenders. Check caps in `references/figures.md`.

**First home buyer stamp duty and grants.** Victoria: full exemption up to one threshold, sliding concession up to a higher one, and the First Home Owner Grant for new homes under a cap. Always confirm current thresholds. For other states, look them up before writing.

**First Home Super Saver (FHSS).** Voluntary super contributions that can be released for a first home deposit, with an annual and total cap. Keep it high level and point to the ATO.

**Refinancing.** Discharge and setup costs, comparison rate vs headline rate, the "loyalty tax" of older loans on higher rates, cashback trade-offs, whether to fix or go variable, and re-assessment risk (the borrower must re-qualify at the buffer rate).

**Investor basics.** Interest-only vs principal-and-interest, offset vs redraw, loan structuring and splitting, equity release, negative gearing in principle (tax is the ATO's domain; keep it general and point to a tax professional), and how rental income is shaded in serviceability.

## Output formats

- **Explainer:** hook line, the plain-English answer, how it works, one hypothetical worked example, common mistakes, the general-information note.
- **Reel script:** 3 hook options, 30 to 45 second script with on-screen text, the general-information note on the final frame.
- **Carousel:** 7 to 10 slides, one idea per slide, cover slide that states the payoff, final slide with a save or follow prompt and the note.
- **Fact-check:** list every claim in the draft, mark each as correct, outdated, wrong or unverifiable with the source, and give the fixed line.

## Style

Follow the Brand Voice section of the context file. Plain language, Australian spelling, metric units, real numbers where sourced, no hype, no guarantees, no em dashes.
