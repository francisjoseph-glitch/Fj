### CAROUSEL COPY: Fixed vs Variable Misconceptions (6 Slides)

**Slide 1 — Hook**
> HEADLINE (8 words): Fixed rates aren't always the safer choice
>
> BODY: Everyone fixes to feel protected. But that protection comes with a cost most borrowers don't read about until it's too late.

**Slide 2 — What most people believe**
> HEADLINE (6 words): The myth: fix when rates fall
>

**Slide 3 — What actually happens**
> HEADLINE (6 words): Break costs can wipe out savings
>
> BODY: Exit a fixed loan early and the bank charges a break cost. Calculated on the Interest Rate Differential. It can reach tens of thousands of dollars.

**Slide 4 — Why it matters**
> HEADLINE (8 words): Life changes. Fixed loan terms do not.
>
> BODY: Selling, refinancing or upsizing during a fixed period triggers break costs. After the RBA's cut to 4.10% in February 2025, those costs have increased for many borrowers. (Source: RBA)

**Slide 5 — What to do about it**
> HEADLINE (6 words): Match your rate type to your timeline
>
> BODY: Staying put for 3 to 5 years? Fixed can work. Likely to sell, refinance or have children? Variable flexibility may be worth more than certainty.

**Slide 6 — CTA**

### CAROUSEL COPY: What Lenders Assess Beyond Income (6 Slides)

**Slide 1 — Hook**
> HEADLINE (7 words): Your salary is not your borrowing power
>
> BODY: Most first home buyers estimate what they can borrow based on income. Lenders run a much stricter calculation than that.

**Slide 2 — What most people believe**
> HEADLINE (7 words): The myth: high income means easy approval
>
> BODY: Strong earnings and clean debts should mean straightforward approval. That logic misses several factors lenders weight heavily in their assessment.

**Slide 3 — What actually happens**
> HEADLINE (7 words): APRA adds 3% to your rate assessment
>
> BODY: The serviceability buffer, set by APRA in October 2021, tests whether you can afford repayments at your current rate plus 3%. A 6% rate becomes a 9% test.

**Slide 4 — Why it matters**
> HEADLINE (7 words): Unused credit limits reduce what you can borrow
>
> BODY: A $20,000 credit card limit, even fully unused, can reduce your borrowing capacity by $80,000 to $100,000. Cards you forgot about still count.

**Slide 5 — What to do about it**
> HEADLINE (6 words): Tidy your credit file before applying
>
> BODY: Close credit cards you don't need. Check BNPL services on your file. Avoid new credit applications in the six months before seeking a home loan.
